The Stories We Build

SN 5.10 · Vajirāsutta

Reflections on Suttas That Stay With Me · 12 August 2026

Who created this sentient being?
Where is its maker?
Where has the being arisen?
And where does it cease?

Who doesn't love to philosophize about life? Probably a lot of people don't. But for most of my life, I enjoyed it. Or maybe enjoyment is the wrong word to describe it, because at times I even took it too far. Always questioning everything. So much that my head would hurt.

I wanted to know why and how the world worked, how and why we move through it as we do, and sometimes the most important thing was to find out who was responsible. Who was responsible for this war, or that conspiracy, or even worse, who was responsible for my suffering. Because that surely could not be me, right?

Over time I started to notice how often these questions depended on very solid ideas about myself and other people. They did this. I was wronged. They should have acted differently. I should not have to feel this way. The stories could become very elaborate, while the actual experience in front of me was usually much simpler: thoughts, feelings, intentions, reactions, all arising because of conditions and changing again.

Vajirā’s reply to Māra points in a different direction. What we call a "being" is not something solid that can be found apart from the conditions that make up our experience. Yet I keep turning myself and other people into fixed characters in the stories I tell about what happens.

When we say “let it go”, it does not mean we should not care. It means that grasping at our ideas on how it should be prevents us from actually caring for what is in front of us.
