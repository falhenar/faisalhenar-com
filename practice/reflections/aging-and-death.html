Aging and Death

SN 3.3 · Jarāmaraṇasutta · 30 July 2026

We cannot escape old age and death, that is why we practice while we can. Let's not waste this life on frivolous things, and let's aim for the good. How much time do I spend pursuing things I already know will not satisfy me very deeply, while neglecting things I genuinely consider important? Even fancy chariots of kings wear out, so why not use this chariot of mine for what I consider worthwhile.

It sounds so easy. But when we are not mindful we usually just act based on our desires and aversions. Rather than simply labeling myself or my actions good or bad, I can look at what my intentions lead to. Do they lead to a more peaceful mind? Do they maybe cause harm to myself or others?

So what I have learned on this path is that I am usually better off grounding my intentions in generosity, restraint and wisdom rather than in greed, aversion and delusion. In my own case, I can maybe find some causes I can donate to on a regular basis and I can decide to practice to gain a clearer understanding of what is actually worth pursuing. This is what 'aiming for the good' has come to mean to me.
