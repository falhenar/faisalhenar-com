The Simile of the Mountain

SN 3.25 · Pabbatūpamasutta

In the Buddha's Words · I.1(2) · 2 August 2026

“I tell you, Great King, I announce to you: old age and death are advancing upon you. Since old age and death are advancing upon you, what would you do?”
“Sir, what can I do but practice the teachings, practice morality, doing skillful and good actions?”

In our own way, we are all kings of our own little kingdoms. So often, we seem to believe that having the right friends, accumulating wealth, clinging to our possessions, or behaving as if we're forever youthful can shield ourselves for what is coming. We all know that we are subject to aging, sickness, and death. No mansion, no family, and no amount of money can protect us from these realities. Even though, on some level, we are all aware of these truths, we continue chasing our desires and running from our aversions. Yet no army can stop the forces of nature or alter the course that every human body is destined to follow.

For many years I too seemed to be in denial about the inevitabilities of this life. I chased dreams of pleasure and happiness that I thought I could enjoy forever. And in that pursuit I often took actions that might have led to some short-term results that seemed pleasurable at the time. But they never led to long-term happiness and fulfilment. I asked myself, what if there were another way? One that might be difficult to walk, not always fun, but that could lead to a life that feels worthy of living.

Becoming aware of the impermanence of everything showed me another path. It freed me from constantly imagining a perfect future that never arrived and allowed me to meet life as it was actually unfolding. This present experience has been shaped by countless causes and conditions; the same will be true of whatever comes next. Knowing the life is finite, why not devote the present moment to cultivating skillful qualities and living with integrity? Such efforts benefit both myself and everyone whose path crosses mine.
