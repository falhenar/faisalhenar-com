What’s the Goal of Skillful Ethics?

AN 10.1 · 17 November 2026

This sutta starts off with the question, “Sir, what is the goal and benefit of skillful ethics?”

I know I always thought of myself as an ethical person. So why does the Buddha emphasize morality and ethics so much? Having spent my early years in a Catholic environment, I often felt ethical behavior was required to avoid punishment. As an adult, I know that way of looking at it might be a bit too simplistic. I believe in most religions, ethics are also taught to support the highest goal, whatever that may be.

What I appreciate in the Buddha’s teachings is that ethics are explained as a logical step in the chain of cause and effect. It is not about some entity rewarding or punishing me for good or bad behavior. I have said things to someone I love in a moment of anger, only to regret afterwards what I said or how I said it. That regret could keep me awake for nights.

It made total sense when I experienced for myself how living more ethically resulted in fewer regrets. And the chain goes on from there. Fewer regrets allow more joy and calm to arise, and those, in turn, create better conditions for deeper practice and understanding.

So I no longer see ethics mainly as a set of rules telling me what I should or should not do. I increasingly see them as the ground from which a calmer and clearer mind can grow. And that, to me, makes ethical living a very practical part of living a happier life.
