The Divine Messengers

from AN 3.36 · Devadūtasutta

In the Buddha's Words · I.1(3) · 9 August 2026

‘My man, because you were negligent, you didn’t do good by way of body, speech, and mind. Well, they’ll definitely punish you to fit your negligence. That bad deed wasn’t done by your mother, father, brother, or sister. It wasn’t done by friends and colleagues, by relatives and kin, by the deities, or by ascetics and brahmins. That bad deed was done by you alone, and you alone will experience the result.’

From a young age I was drawn to American hip-hop. I was enchanted by the stories the artists told about sex, crime and life in poverty, but also by the social issues they seemed to shine a light on. Some of these artists I really looked up to, even though my own life looked nothing like theirs.

Then, in the mid-nineties, two famous rappers I loved listening to were killed. Looking back, their deaths could have been divine messengers for me. The message was there, but I didn't receive it. For years afterwards I continued to look up to rappers and to see their wealth and status as something worth striving for, while overlooking behavior that now seems clearly at odds with how I understand morality.

Maybe it was getting older myself, and eventually growing older than either of those two rappers ever became, that started to make the message more real. This life is fleeting. And looking back, I can also see that I have lived much of my life in abundance without really realizing it. There were times when I had little money, but I cannot say that I have ever truly lived in poverty. Yet that abundance has never brought me very much happiness. Slowly, perhaps, I started to understand what those divine messengers had been telling me all along.

And then there was my trip to Wat Chom Tong. I saw monks devoting their lives to practice, while giving thousands of lay people the opportunity to learn and practice themselves. For me, that was something different. Aging and death had begun to show me that my time was limited; at Wat Chom Tong I saw something worthwhile to do with that time.

It showed me that, even though walking this path is not easy, it seems worth trying.
