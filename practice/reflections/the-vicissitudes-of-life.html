The Vicissitudes of Life

AN 8.6

In the Buddha's Words · I.2(2) · 31 August 2026

Gain and loss, fame and disgrace,
blame and praise, and pleasure and pain.
These qualities among mankind are impermanent,
transient, and perishable.
An intelligent and mindful person knows these things,
seeing that they’re perishable.
Desirable things don’t disturb their mind,
nor are they repelled by the undesirable.

This sutta concerns the eight worldly conditions, sometimes called ‘the eight worldly winds’. The word wind immediately gives this image of being at risk to be swept away by it. And that is exactly what seems to happen to us when we get into contact with these eight worldly conditions of gain and loss, fame and disgrace, blame and praise, pleasure and pain.

For myself, I know I am always eager to receive praise of some kind for the things that I do. Whether it’s doing my job at the office or when I do something nice for my wife. Receiving praise in those instances can light up my day and make me feel good about myself. And it took me a while to see the other side of that coin. Being dependent on praise makes you correspondingly vulnerable to blame.

Recently I have had a couple of incidents where I felt someone blamed me for doing, or even not doing something. And it stung. Not only that, I carried it with me for a couple of days. Thinking about it to myself and, even worse, debating with the other person. All inside this head of mine. Concocting different stories and scenarios to explain why I wasn’t really to blame, or why the other person might also have carried some responsibility. Not realizing I was being swept away by this worldly wind.

I took me a few days to see for myself what was happening in this mind. And to notice that although the unpleasant feeling of blame and the pleasant feeling of praise feel very different, they share the same nature. Both arise and cease. Both are subject to change. And when I get caught up in either wanting one or resisting the other, both can become a source of suffering.
