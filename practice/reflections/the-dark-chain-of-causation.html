The Dark Chain of Causation

from DN 15 · 2 November 2026

This part of the anthology is just a short excerpt from a long discourse; DN 15, ‘The Great Discourse on Causation’. Monks, scholars, and practitioners far more advanced and dedicated than I am have studied this discourse for more than two thousand years. Whole books and many talks have been devoted to it, so I don’t imagine this reflection will explain how to understand the chain of dependent origination, and neither is that my intention.

What I can relate it to is my own experience. My own practice is primarily based on the four foundations of mindfulness, where feeling is thought of as the second foundation. What could be the reason for mindfulness of feeling being the second foundation? Is it that when being mindful of feeling, we are better equipped to observe what comes after feeling arises?

As the excerpt says, “in dependence upon feeling there is craving; in dependence upon craving there is pursuit … various evil unwholesome things originate.” As I understand it, when we go along with feelings, we risk ending up with unwholesome mind states. Mind states that can lead us to perform unskillful actions.

But when I note the feeling the moment it arises, it sometimes seems to stop this chain before the next link forms. Whether it is the unpleasant feeling of being blamed or the pleasant feeling of being praised, if I catch it in the right moment, I am not swept away by one of the eight worldly winds.
