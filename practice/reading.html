Reading

A shelf of books that have stayed with me, and a few I would hand someone just starting out.

This is a personal shelf, not a bibliography or a curriculum. It holds books I would feel comfortable putting in someone else's hands, chosen slowly and changed as I keep reading. A book listed here isn't always an unqualified endorsement: some I would recommend without hesitation, some come with a caveat worth knowing, and a few are here mainly because they mattered to me at a particular time.

Most of these books are free to read online. Where a book has to be bought, the entry says so.

Books I return to

Books I've actually reopened, not just liked once and put back on the shelf.

The Four Noble Truths ↗

Ajahn Sumedho

This book came with me on an e-reader during a trip to Thailand, when I was looking for a way through the monkey mind that had held me in a chokehold for much of my life, perhaps all of it. Together with my experience at my first Goenka retreat in Suriname, it gave me faith that this path could genuinely lead to the lessening, and perhaps eventually the overcoming, of suffering.

Free · Abhayagiri Monastery

Bodhinyana ↗

Ajahn Chah

This book came with me when I visited Suddhavari Monastery in Brazil. Staying at a monastery in Ajahn Chah's lineage, I felt deeply grateful for another opportunity to deepen the practice. My time there taught me a great deal about practising mindfulness during ordinary duties, and this book became part of that experience.

Free · Forest Sangha

Practical Insight Meditation: Basic and Progressive Stages ↗

Mahāsi Sayādaw

Ajahn Tong's approach belongs to the wider Mahāsi tradition. This short text helped me see more clearly the lineage behind the meditation instructions I had received. It sits alongside the books in Meditation & Practice.

Free · Buddhist University · BPS BP503S

New to Buddhist reading?

A possible way in, not a required one. Three books, in a gentle order.

  1. 1

    The Four Noble Truths ↗

    Ajahn Sumedho

    A brief orientation to the central framework.

    Free · Abhayagiri Monastery · why I return to it

  2. 2

    Mindfulness in Plain English ↗

    Bhante Henepola Gunaratana

    Practical meditation instruction in accessible language.

    Free · Buddhist University

  3. 3

    The Art of Living: Peace and Freedom in the Here and Now ↗

    Thich Nhat Hanh

    A gentler treatment of awareness in ordinary life. Thich Nhat Hanh writes from the Zen tradition rather than the Theravada teachers above, so the register is different. It is also the one book in this sequence you have to buy.

    Buy · HarperCollins

Main shelves

Meditation & Practice

Books to sit with, not just read once. Practical Insight Meditation belongs here too.

The Only Way: Path to Nibbana

Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo

Since visiting Wat Chom Tong Vipassana Meditation Center in 2023, these instructions have stayed with me in daily practice. The book has since been revised and republished, but the words I encountered then still remain with me.

Wat Chom Tong · no edition online; usually given at the monastery

On Meditation ↗

Ajahn Chah

Although the formal teachings I receive come through Ajahn Tong's lineage, I am also deeply moved by Ajahn Chah's lineage. The manner of teaching is different, and I feel fortunate that both have crossed my path.

Free · Forest Sangha

Mindfulness: The Path to the Deathless ↗

Ajahn Sumedho

Instructions on awareness in posture, feeling, and daily activity.

Free · Forest Sangha

The Heart of Buddhist Meditation ↗

Nyanaponika Thera

A study of satipaṭṭhāna, first published by the Buddhist Publication Society.

Free · Buddho.org · BPS BP509S

In This Very Life ↗

Sayādaw U Paṇḍita

Another teacher in the Mahāsi lineage, Sayādaw U Paṇḍita helped me understand more clearly what sustained vipassanā practice can look like in daily life.

Free · Buddho.org · BPS BP508S

A Householder's Vinaya ↗

Allan Cooper

Allan Cooper wrote the kind of book I had been looking for. After a retreat, I can quickly fall back into old habits. This book offers practical tools for carrying a measure of ethics, concentration and wisdom back into life at home.

Free PDF · householdersvinaya.com

Lives

I have always been drawn to biographies, and that did not change when I came to Buddhist practice. I find encouragement in seeing the effort other people have made, and in the lives they have offered as a guiding light for others. I am especially grateful to Ajaan Dick Sīlaratano and to the translators who have made these stories available to us.

Gold Wrapped in Rags ↗

Ajaan Jia Cundo, translated by Ajaan Dick Sīlaratano

The autobiography of a forest monk who trained under Ajaan Mun and practiced alongside Ajaan Mahā Boowa, told in his own words.

Free · Forest Dhamma

Venerable Ācariya Mun Bhūridatta Thera ↗

Ajaan Mahā Boowa Ñāṇasampanno, translated by Ajaan Dick Sīlaratano

Ajaan Mahā Boowa's account of his own teacher, Ajaan Mun, a central figure of the modern Thai Forest Tradition.

Free · Forest Dhamma

Samana ↗

Ajaan Mahā Boowa Ñāṇasampanno, edited by Ajaan Dick Sīlaratano

A short account of Ajaan Mahā Boowa's final days and passing, written for those who practiced with him.

Free · Forest Dhamma

Uncommon Wisdom ↗

Ajaan Dick Sīlaratano

The life of Ajaan Paññāvaḍḍho, an English-born monk who trained for decades under Ajaan Mahā Boowa in the northeastern Thai forest tradition.

Free · Forest Dhamma

Mae Chee Kaew ↗

Ajaan Dick Sīlaratano

The life of a laywoman turned nun in rural northeastern Thailand who studied under several of the era's best-known meditation teachers.

Free · Forest Dhamma
