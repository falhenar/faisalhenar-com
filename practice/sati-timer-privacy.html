Sati Timer privacy

What the app stores, what it sends, and why it asks for what it asks for.

Effective 4 September 2026

Sati Timer collects nothing about you. There is no account to make, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking of any kind. Nothing you do in the app is reported to me or to anyone else, and there is nothing for me to look at even if I wanted to.

The rest of this page says the same thing in more detail, because a policy that only says “we respect your privacy” tells you nothing you can check.

What the app stores

Everything the app remembers is kept in its own private storage on your phone, where no other app can read it:

Your timer presets and the settings you last used — durations, bells, volume, the lead-in, whether a session repeats.

Your reading: which passages you have bookmarked, where you stopped reading in each sutta, and whether the Pali is shown.

A session in progress, so that a sit survives the app being closed or stopped by your phone.

Whether you have dismissed the note about background restrictions.

That is the entire list. There is no name, no email address, no identifier, no contact list, no location and no record of when or how long you sat. Uninstalling the app removes all of it.

What the app sends

Nothing. Sati Timer makes no network requests of its own — not on first launch, not in the background, not ever. The suttas, the instructions, the diagrams and the bell sounds are all bundled into the app itself, which is why it works with no connection at all.

The only time the internet is involved is when you tap a link — to SuttaCentral, to this site, or to the Apache licence text on the About screen. Those open in your own browser, and from that point you are on someone else's website under their terms, not the app's.

Android's own backup

Android offers to back apps' settings up to your Google account. Sati Timer allows this, so if you have that feature switched on, your presets and settings may travel to a new phone with the rest of your backup.

That backup is yours and lives in your Google account. It does not pass through me and I have no access to it. If you would rather it did not happen, Android's own backup settings control it.

Why the app asks for what it asks for

Notifications, so the running sit can show how long is left and you can return to it.

Alarms and reminders, so a bell rings at the moment it is due even when the screen is off and the phone is asleep. This is how the timer works at all; without it the bells would arrive late or not at all.

Playing audio in the background, for the few seconds a bell is actually sounding.

It asks for nothing else. No microphone, no camera, no location, no contacts, no access to your files, no reading of other apps.

Sati Timer is not made for children.

It is not directed at children and is not designed for them. Since it collects no personal information from anyone, it collects none from children either.

If this changes

If a future version ever collected or sent anything, this page would say so before that version was released, and the change would be described here rather than quietly folded in. The date at the top is when this page was last true.

Questions about any of it are welcome — the contact page is the way to reach me.

Sati Timer is a meditation timer for noting practice in the style taught by Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo, following the meditation manual of Yuttadhammo Bhikkhu. It is made by one person and given away.
