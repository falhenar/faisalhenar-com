Watching

A short list of channels worth watching: mostly recorded talks and guided meditation, chosen for substance over production value. Roughly arranged from where to start to where it goes from there.

Just beginning

For people who are curious about Buddhism or meditation.

Buddha Speaks

The basics, explained simply

Short explanations of core Buddhist teachings in plain English. A good place to send someone just starting out, not a deep well for long-time practitioners.

Watch →

Doug's Dharma

Doug Kraft, early Buddhist teachings, secular framing

Short videos relating early Buddhist teachings to ordinary life, from a scholarly but plainspoken, secular angle rather than a devotional one.

Watch →

Buddhist Society of Western Australia

Ajahn Brahm and the sangha at Bodhinyana and Dhammasara Monasteries, Perth

Dhamma talks recorded at Dhammaloka Buddhist Centre: plainspoken and often funny, and a common first stop for people new to meditation.

Watch →
Deepening practice

For people who already have an established meditation practice.

Amaravati Buddhist Monastery

Ajahn Amaro and the sangha, Thai Forest Tradition, England

The monastery Ajahn Sumedho founded in 1984, in the same forest lineage as Ajahn Chah. Less introductory than some others here: closer to sitting in on the actual life of the place.

Watch →

Ajahn Achalo Bhikkhu

Guided meditations and Dhamma talks

Step-by-step guided meditations alongside sutta readings and talks from contemporary teachers, built as a plain, practical resource rather than a polished production.

Watch →

Clear Mountain Monastery Project

Ajahn Kovilo, Ajahn Nisabho, and friends (Thai Forest tradition, Seattle)

Recorded talks from a small monastery still taking root in the Ajahn Chah forest lineage. Informal and unhurried, clearly made for a small sangha rather than an audience.

Watch →
Beyond Theravāda

Teachers from other Buddhist traditions I've personally found worthwhile.

Tergar Meditation Community

Led by Yongey Mingyur Rinpoche (Karma Kagyu lineage, Tibetan Buddhism)

Meditation instruction built around Mingyur Rinpoche's "Joy of Living" curriculum: Tibetan in lineage, taught in plain, often funny language that leans on modern science as much as tradition.

Watch →

James Low

Dzogchen and Tibetan Buddhist teachings

A different tradition than the rest of this list (Tibetan Dzogchen rather than Theravada), but recorded seminars worth the time regardless of lineage.
