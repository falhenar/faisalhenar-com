    In the Buddha's Words

    Aging and Death

    SN 3.3 · Jarāmaraṇasutta · I.1(1)

    We cannot escape old age and death, that is why we practice while we can. Let's not waste this life on frivolous things, and let's aim for the good. How much time do I spend pursuing things I already know will not satisfy me very deeply, while neglecting things I genuinely consider important?

    The Dart of Painful Feeling

    SN 36.6 · Sallasutta · I.2(1)

    We will all experience suffering in our lives in one form or another. In that sense, getting struck by the "first arrow" is unavoidable. We age, we get sick and, at some point, we will all die. Along the way there will inevitably be painful and uncomfortable feelings.

    In the Buddha's Words

    The Vicissitudes of Life

    AN 8.6 · I.2(2)

    This sutta concerns the eight worldly conditions, sometimes called ‘the eight worldly winds’. The word wind immediately gives this image of being at risk to be swept away by it. And that is exactly what seems to happen to us when we get into contact with these eight worldly conditions of gain and loss, fame and disgrace, blame and praise, pleasure and pain.
