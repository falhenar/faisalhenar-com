Teachers

Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo

Former abbot, Wat Chom Tong, Chiang Mai

Read a short bio

Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo was born in 1923 and ordained as a novice in 1934, receiving full ordination a decade later. In the 1950s he was sent by the Northern Thai Sangha to Wat Mahathat in Bangkok to study the four foundations of mindfulness, followed by two years teaching and practicing in Burma alongside the Venerable Mahasi Sayadaw. He went on to become a widely respected meditation teacher in Thailand, leading Vipassana courses across Europe and America from the early 1980s onward. Most of his life and teaching was based at Wat Phradhatu Sri Chom Tong, a large, well-known meditation monastery near Chiang Mai, which he headed as abbot and which includes an international center for foreign practitioners.

Ajahn Poonsak Suwadhano

Whom I consider my primary teacher

Read a short bio

A disciple of Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo, Ajahn Poonsak teaches Vipassana meditation at Wat Chom Tong, continuing the tradition established there. Students who have practiced under him describe a patient, unhurried teaching style: attentive to where each person actually is in their practice, rather than a fixed curriculum.

Monasteries & Meditation Centers

Wat Chom Tong

Vipassana Meditation Centre, Chiang Mai

Founded in 1991, teaching the four foundations of mindfulness through sitting, walking, and prostration practice. Home to Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo as former abbot, and to Ajahn Poonsak Suwadhano, whom I consider my primary teacher. Courses open to both Thai and international students.

Casa de Meditación Vipassana

Meditation center, Mexico City

Theravada meditation in the northern Thai lineage, founded by a student of Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo. Workshops, group sits, and silent retreats from 3 to 20 days, in person and online.

Mosteiro Suddhavāri

Monastery, Brazil (Theravada Forest Tradition)

Connected to the Sociedade Budista do Brasil, offering teachings, chanting, and meditation guidance entirely free of charge, supported by donation. Recorded talks, a digital library, and accommodation for visitors.
