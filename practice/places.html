Places & Community

The teachers, monasteries, and community that have shaped this practice.

  • Teachers

    Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo

    Former abbot, Wat Chom Tong, Chiang Mai

    Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo was born in 1923 and ordained as a novice in 1934, receiving full ordination a decade later. In the 1950s he was sent by the Northern Thai Sangha to Wat Mahathat in Bangkok to study the four foundations of mindfulness, followed by two years teaching and practicing in Burma alongside the Venerable Mahasi Sayadaw. He went on to become a widely respected meditation teacher in Thailand, leading Vipassana courses across Europe and America from the early 1980s onward. Most of his life and teaching was based at Wat Phradhatu Sri Chom Tong, a large, well-known meditation monastery near Chiang Mai, which he headed as abbot and which includes an international center for foreign practitioners.

  • Teachers

    Ajahn Poonsak Suwadhano

    Whom I consider my primary teacher

    A disciple of Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo, Ajahn Poonsak taught me during a 40-day retreat at Wat Chom Tong in 2023. His teaching style is patient and unhurried: attentive to where I actually was in my practice, rather than a fixed curriculum. May whatever merit I make in my practice be dedicated to him.

  • Chiang Mai, Thailand
    Places I've Practiced

    Wat Chom Tong

    Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo's lineage

    Founded in 1991, teaching the four foundations of mindfulness through sitting, walking, and prostration practice. Home to Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo as the late abbot, and to Ajahn Poonsak Suwadhano, whom I consider my primary teacher. This is where I sat my longest retreat so far, and it changed the course of my life: looking back at where I was a few years ago, the difference is night and day. The meditation instructions on this site follow that same method, taught by Yuttadhammo Bhikkhu.

    Thai and international students · No set fee: dana at the end of your stay

    Casa de Meditación Vipassana

    Ajahn Tong lineage

    Theravada meditation in the northern Thai lineage, founded by a student of Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo. Unlike most centers in this lineage, retreats here carry a set fee rather than pure donation, since the group doesn't have a permanent space of its own and rents one for each retreat. Teachers from Wat Chom Tong visit regularly; in 2024 I had the chance to practice with Ajahn Poonsak again, when he came over from Thailand.

    Retreats of 3 to 20 days, in person and online · Set fee for lodging, meals and parking; transport not included

    Casa de Meditación Vipassana ↗
  • Brazil
    Places I've Practiced

    Mosteiro Suddhavāri

    Theravada Forest Tradition

    Connected to the Sociedade Budista do Brasil, offering teachings, chanting, and meditation guidance. Recorded talks, a digital library, and accommodation for visitors. I stayed here for ten days in 2025 and learned a great deal about carrying mindfulness into daily activities, thanks to Ajahn Mudito and everyone who supports the monastery.

    Free, including lodging · Sustained by donation

    Mosteiro Suddhavāri ↗

  • Suriname
    Where I Started

    Vipassana Suriname

    S.N. Goenka tradition

    Local practitioners who organize Vipassana courses in the S.N. Goenka tradition, usually at a temporary retreat site rather than a permanent center. This is where I did my first retreat, and I'm grateful for that opportunity, though the technique and structure differ somewhat from the tradition I practice in now. They also run a Facebook page.

    Ten-day courses, roughly every other year · No fee: donation after the course, if you are able

    Vipassana Suriname ↗
