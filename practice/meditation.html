Meditation

Also available in Dutch

Instructions and tools for meditation practice. Texts I come back to often, and apps to use daily.

I've benefited a great deal from the teachers I've met along this path, particularly the instructions I received from Ajahn Poonsak at Wat Chom Tong, in the lineage of Ajahn Tong. If you're serious about deepening your practice I'd recommend finding a teacher who can guide you. We live in a time when information is readily available online, which is genuinely useful, but it doesn't compare to practicing under an established teacher.

That said, sometimes circumstances don't allow for practicing with a teacher. In those cases, texts and meditation apps can be a good way to learn, though it takes real effort and determination on your own. It also helps to find others willing to talk, share, and practice together. If you'd ever like to talk about practice, feel free to reach out through the contact page.

Meditation instructions

Within the Buddhist tradition there are multiple ways in which meditation is taught. In the Theravada tradition these are all derived from the pali canon and their commentaries. The meditation instructions you will find here are in line with the formal teachings I have received from Ajahn Poonsak in the lineage of Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo. The instructions here are translated and written by Yuttadhammo Bhikkhu, whose recorded teaching is also on Watching. Dutch translations of the three sheets below are available as PDF downloads.

How to Meditate ↗

Yuttadhammo Bhikkhu, sirimangalo.org

The full manual these instructions are drawn from, as taught by Ajahn Tong Sirimangalo.

English PDF · Dutch translation

Sitting meditation

Sheet 1 of 3

Watching the rise and fall of the abdomen, and noting whatever else takes the mind away from it.

Walking meditation

Sheet 2 of 3

A short straight path, walked slowly, with the note kept in time with the foot.

Mindful prostration

Sheet 3 of 3

Three prostrations, made slowly, with every movement noted. The practice that opens a session.

Meditation apps

Waking Up ↗

Sam Harris

A secular meditation app pairing guided practice with talks on mindfulness, philosophy, and neuroscience. This was my first entrance into meditation, and I still return to it often for the conversations and the theory sections. Many traditions are represented here, which makes it a good, slightly intellectual way to get a first sense of different practices. Though it's taught from an explicitly secular stance, distinct from the Theravada framing of the rest of this page. It's a paid subscription; a free one is available through the scholarship program.

Insight Timer ↗

Insight Timer

A free meditation app I've used for years. You can set your own times and bells, so the timer is fully customizable to what you need. It also has a large library of guided sessions and talks I haven't really explored myself, though feel free to. The timer is free; a paid subscription unlocks more of the library.
